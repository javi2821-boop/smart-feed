---
type: lesson
title: You become what you practice
character: philosopher
background: open-field
source: Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book II (paraphrased)
---
Aristotle noticed something odd about good character. We do not become brave first and then act bravely. He argued it runs the other way. We become builders by building and harpists by playing the harp, and in the same way we become just by doing just things and brave by doing brave things. A virtue is less a feeling you wait for and more a skill you practice badly until it is yours. That changes the question on a hard day. Not, am I a patient person? Only, what would a patient person do in the next five minutes? Do that, and you are building one.
