title: Pride needs someone to beat
character: old-man

source: C.S. Lewis, Mere Christianity (paraphrased)
---
In Mere Christianity, C. S. Lewis called pride the great sin, and he gave a way to spot it. He argued that pride is competitive by its very nature. It takes no pleasure in having something, only in having more of it than the next person. We say someone is proud of being rich, or clever, or good looking. Lewis said that is not quite it. They are proud of being richer, cleverer, or better looking than the others in the room. Take away the comparison and the pleasure goes with it. So here is a test drawn from that. Think of the thing you are most pleased with about yourself. Would it still please you if everyone had it?
