---
type: lesson
title: What you attend to
character: teacher
background: study
source: William James, The Principles of Psychology, 1890 (paraphrased)
---
In eighteen ninety, the psychologist William James wrote that his experience was whatever he agreed to attend to. Not what happened around him. What he noticed. Two people can sit in the same room for an hour and live two different hours, because each mind was built out of what it looked at. James thought the things we never notice simply do not shape us at all. That makes attention the closest thing you have to a steering wheel. Whatever gets the next ten minutes of your looking is, in a small way, becoming you. So before you pick something up, ask what it is going to build.
