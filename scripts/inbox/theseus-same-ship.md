The Greek writer Plutarch tells of a ship the Athenians kept for centuries, the one Theseus sailed home in. As each plank rotted, they replaced it with new timber. In time, not one original board was left. Plutarch says the philosophers were already arguing about it. One side said it was the same ship. The other said it clearly was not. Now bring it closer. Most of the cells in your body have been replaced since you were a child, and so have many of your opinions, your habits, even what you want. Something has carried through all of it, or nothing has. So what, exactly, makes you the same person you were ten years ago?
