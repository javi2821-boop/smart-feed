In September nineteen sixty-five, a Navy pilot named James Stockdale was shot down over North Vietnam. He later said that as he floated down in his parachute, with about thirty seconds before he landed in a village, he told himself he was leaving the world of technology and entering the world of Epictetus. He had studied the old Stoic years before, almost by accident. He was a prisoner for more than seven years, and he was often tortured. What he carried in was one distinction: what was up to him, and what was not. His captors had his body and his days. They never got his choices. What are you studying now that you might need later?
